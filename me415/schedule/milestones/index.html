Milestone #1: Overview

Read through the project overview and the milestones on this page.

Milestone #2: Team Selection / Configuration

Create a team of 4-5 and submit your team name and the names of the members of your team.

In a sentence describe the type of plane you are planning on. For example traditional wing/tail, canard, flying wing, biplane, foam wing, built-up wing with monokote, composite layup, etc. Note that in all milestones, you aren’t locked into any design decisions.

Milestone #3: Airfoil/Wing Design

Submit a maximum 2-page memo describing your airfoil and wing design including justification for your design decisions backed by analysis.

Milestone #4: Full Aircraft

Have a complete aircraft design with design decisions backed by analysis. Nothing to turn in, rather will discuss it at the design review. We will split team into three groups between myself and the two TAs. Your team will have 12 minutes for the in-class review. The grade is based on providing sufficient information to understand the design but is not based on the quality of the aircraft design or on presentation quality. The purpose of the review is to have a dialogue to allow for helpful feedback and answering of questions. In other words, you don’t need to spend time making fancy slides, but you do need to present key information clearly so that we can have a meaningful discussion. At a minimum, I recommend including the following:

  • Multiple views of your design and your overarching configuration rationale.
  • Estimated total weight, the weight of key components, and the center of mass location.
  • Estimated lift to drag ratio.
  • Design speed and stall speed.
  • Airfoil design and rationale including lift curve, drag polar, and moment curve.
  • Wing design and rationale including lift and cl distributions.
  • Tail and Fuselage design and rationale including open loop stability plot, static margin, and key stability derivatives.
  • Any other unique design aspects.

Milestone #5: Built Part

Bring a built part to class. It can be anything: a wing you cut on the foam cutter, a 3D printed part for a wing/fuselage junction, a prototype cardboard version of the plane, a servo with control surface and linkage for practice, etc.

Milestone #6: Electronics selection

Submit a maximum 2-page memo describing your battery, motor, prop selection including justification for your design decisions backed by analysis. After you are satisfied with your selection (talk to us if not sure), you should buy your components.

Milestone #7: Build Review

Have a complete aircraft (minus electronics). You won’t be penalized if you have less than a full aircraft, but if you are behind you should be ready to discuss clear plans for completion. Similar format to last time. The main focus will be on providing feedback and suggestions on your build.

Milestone #8: Glide Test

Subnit a video of a glide test, and your observed lift-to-drag ratio.

Milestone #9: Transmitter Binding

Checkoff with TAs that your control surface work and you have a profile saved on the transmitter.

Final Report

Report in PDF format. The cover page should contain your team name, the names of all team members, and a nice photo (or rendering) of your aircraft.
