Problem 2b — Why does a transport step-climb?

An electric RC aircraft mass stays effectively constant as a battery discharges. A long-range transport, on the other hand, may burn a substantial fraction of its mass during cruise. In this problem we will investigate how that changes the desired cruise altitude.

Suppose the transport burns fuel until its mass is 170,000 kg while its geometry and configuration remain unchanged. If it stays at 10,000 m and Mach 0.85, calculate its new required lift coefficient.

A simple idealized cruise schedule instead holds Mach number and lift coefficient at their beginning-of-cruise values. Using the ideal-gas and speed-of-sound relationships. you can show that dynaic pressure is

\[q=\frac{1}{2}\rho(Ma)^2=\frac{\gamma}{2}pM^2,\]

where $\gamma$ is the ratio of specific heats and has a value of 1.4 for air. Thus, at fixed Mach number, dynamic pressure is proportional to atmospheric pressure. Calculate the altitude at the end of cruise when the mass has decreased to 170,000 kg (in order to maintain the initial lift coefficient).

Consider why a real transport follows discrete step climbs rather than a continuous ideal schedule.
